03 — Prompting Philosophy

Directing machines like a crew.

Good AI video isn't luck. It comes from giving the engine the same precise instructions a director gives a camera operator, a continuity supervisor and a DP.

01

Camera Control

I treat every generation as a shot on a shot list. Instead of only describing what should appear, I describe how the camera moves through it — a slow orbit around the product, a low-angle push-in, a lateral pan across the skyline, a tracking shot that holds the subject at frame-left. Explicit lens, angle and movement language turns a random clip into a directed shot, and makes the edit cut together cleanly.

02

Scene Consistency

AI engines forget everything between generations, so continuity has to be engineered. I lock a reference set first — character sheets, product renders and a fixed style frame — then reuse the same reference images and descriptors across every tool in the pipeline. Keyframes built in MidJourney carry into the video engines, and upscaling keeps detail uniform shot to shot, so a face, a bottle or a room reads as the same asset from first frame to last.

03

Mood & Lighting

Lighting is where AI footage either looks cinematic or looks generated. I specify it the way a cinematographer would — volumetric haze, a motivated key light, rim light separating subject from background, deep contrast with protected highlights — then grade toward one consistent palette in the edit. Audio gets the same intent: voice and ambience are timed to the cut so the sound carries the mood instead of just filling silence.
